How it works

Evacusense lays real satellite fire detections over the real road network, step by step (every 3 hours), and estimates which roads and how many residents were likely cut off from every exit.

Data

  • Fire detections: NASA FIRMS (VIIRS and MODIS satellites)
  • Satellite imagery: Copernicus Sentinel-2 cloudless, from EOX (2025 version, so it shows the land before the fire)
  • Roads and places: OpenStreetMap
  • Population: WorldPop 100 m grid (2025 estimate, modelled, not a census)
  • Briefings: written by an AI model from the numbers above

How well it matches

Checked against the official BC Wildfire Service fire perimeter: 97% of detections fall inside it, and 87% of its area has a detection within 1 km. Road closures and drive times are not validated.

Limits

  • Satellites only see fire when they pass overhead, hours apart.
  • “Likely affected” means within 100 m of a detection. It is not an official closure list.
  • Drive times are estimated minimums (free-flow, no congestion) from sample points.
  • The official Highway 97 closure was longer than what the map marks. No detections were found near Peachland.
  • The AI briefings have not been reviewed by native speakers.

Not official guidance. This is an illustrative planning tool built from public data. For real evacuations follow BC Wildfire Service, EmergencyInfoBC and local evacuation orders.

About these figures▾

Residents come from WorldPop 100 m gridded population (2025 estimate, approximate), with each cell’s people assigned to the nearest road node; “cut off” means no drivable route to an exit once likely-affected roads are removed. Drive times are estimated minimum times (free-flow, no congestion) using OSM speed limits where tagged (about 15% of roads) and default speeds otherwise (from 74 sample points in Summerland). Areas are 1 km squares: “cut off +9.4 h” means that many hours after the first satellite detection, most of the area’s residents had lost every route to an exit. Satellites only see fire when they pass over, and no detections were found near Peachland.

Figures are estimates from public data, not an official evacuation order or guidance. Follow BC Wildfire Service, EmergencyInfoBC and local authorities.
